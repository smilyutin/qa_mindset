**Model for Improvement: Define the Aim, Measure and Test**

---

## Definition

MFI — the Model for Improvement — asks three questions:

1. What are we trying to accomplish?
2. How will we know a change is an improvement?
3. What change could produce that improvement?

PDSA cycles test the proposed changes.

---

## Relevant Example

> “I would work with staff and service users to define an aim for reducing avoidable delays, select measures, and test changes such as clearer intake guidance.”

| Question | Apply it to intake delays |
| --- | --- |
| What are we trying to accomplish? | Agree whose delays to reduce, what support they need, and a measurable aim with a timeframe. |
| How will we know a change is an improvement? | Track time until support, completeness at first submission, and the workload and effort involved. |
| What change could produce that improvement? | Develop clearer intake guidance with staff and service users, then test it on a small scale. |

Use a [PDSA cycle](#/modules/pdsa) to record a prediction, trial the guidance, compare results with the prediction, and decide whether to adopt, adapt or abandon the change.

---

## Measures

| Type | Measure | What it helps us understand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome | Time until the person receives the intended support. | Whether people receive support sooner. |
| Process | Percentage of requests complete at first submission. | Whether the intake process is working as intended. |
| Balancing | Staff workload and the effort required from individuals and families. | Whether an improvement creates additional burden elsewhere. |

Agree when timing starts and ends, what counts as a complete request, and how workload and participant effort will be recorded. Establish a baseline and review results over time, including differences between groups and types of request.

---

## My Experience

> “I have translated requirements into acceptance criteria, coordinated validation, and reported quality risks. These skills support defining aims and assessing results, although I would not claim formal MFI implementation experience.”

QA experience provides practical skills for clarifying the intended result, coordinating a test and examining evidence. Keep the distinction clear between transferable skills and experience formally implementing MFI.

---

## Service-User Involvement and Unintended Effects

> “Service users should help define the aim and what success looks like. I would assess whether the change improves their experience, rather than simply improving an internal target.”

Involve individuals and families in choosing the aim, reviewing the guidance and interpreting the trial results. Ask whether the process feels clearer, more accessible and less burdensome.

Check whether:

- Faster internal processing leads to earlier receipt of the intended support.
- Clearer guidance reduces confusion without making applications harder to complete.
- Work is shifted from staff to individuals or families.
- Some groups face longer waits, additional barriers or greater effort.
- People stop applying or leave the process before receiving support.

Use this feedback alongside the measures when deciding what to test next.

---

## How the Improvement Approaches Connect

| Approach | Focus in this example |
| --- | --- |
| MFI | Define the aim, measures and changes worth testing. |
| PDSA | Test a proposed change and learn from the results. |
| Lean | Examine waiting, handoffs and rework across the intake journey. |
| Six Sigma | Examine variation and recurring causes of incomplete requests or delays. |

Use the approaches together where they help answer the improvement question. Record the aim, measures, proposed change and learning from each trial.
